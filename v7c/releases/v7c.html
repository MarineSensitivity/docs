4  v7c — sea turtles and the North Atlantic right whale where the record places them

v7c, titled v7.2, is the second surgical patch of the public v7 release, built the way v7b was. It is not a new build: v7’s database is cloned, the merged models of six sea turtles and the North Atlantic right whale are edited under their existing model ids, and v7’s own scoring is re-executed verbatim. It replaces v7b as the version under review. Three rules change, and nothing else does:

  1. The turtle record rule. A sea-turtle species is dropped from an Alaska ecoregion where it has never been recorded through any observational channel: sightings, strandings, bycatch, fishery observers, survey and opportunistic records. This replaces v7b’s suitability threshold.
  2. The right whale Gulf rule. The North Atlantic right whale is dropped from the two Gulf of America ecoregions, which lie outside its range.
  3. The coverage floor κ, carried over unchanged from v7b.

Productivity, the national score and everything else planned for v10 are untouched. Every primary-productivity row of v7c equals v7’s, and a build gate checks this in every run.

ImportantServed on the review host only

v7c is a restricted pre-release. It is served only through the signed-in review host: the Atlas (Scores lens, Species lens) and this documentation. It is never what a reader gets by default, and promoting it is a separate decision.

4.1 Three rules, each off in the control run

Each rule is a flag in the build notebook, and each flag’s control value is off. With all three off, the same code reproduces v7 exactly: the same cells, the same values and the same scores. That gate makes every difference below attributable to one rule. The build also runs each rule on its own, so the Program-Area table further down shows what each rule moves.

4.2 The rules as equations

4.2.1 A cell belongs to one ecoregion

Ecoregions are the unit of exclusion because they are the frame the score is rescaled in (Equation 10.4), and because the evidence is stated at that scale (“never recorded in the Bering Sea”; “the Gulf is outside its range”). A cell that straddles two ecoregions belongs to the one covering most of it:

\[ e(c) = \underset{e}{\arg\max}\; p_{c,e} \tag{4.1}\]

where \(p_{c,e}\) is the percentage of cell \(c\) covered by ecoregion \(e\). A tie goes to the ecoregion whose key sorts first, so the assignment is deterministic (msens::cell_zone_major_sql()).

4.2.2 Rule 1: the turtle record rule

For species \(s\), Alaska ecoregion \(e\) (Gulf of Alaska, East Bering Sea, Chukchi and Beaufort Seas, High Arctic) and observational channel \(k\), let \(n_{s,e,k}\) be the number of records of \(s\) in \(e\) from channel \(k\), and \(K_e\) the channels that reach \(e\). Let \(R_s\) be the cells of the species’ v7 merged model. The species is dropped from the ecoregion when no channel has ever recorded it there and v7 gives it range there:

\[ \text{drop}_{s,e} \;=\; \mathbb{1}\!\left[\sum_{k \in K_e} n_{s,e,k} = 0\right] \cdot \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,\lvert \{c \in R_s : e(c) = e\} \rvert \ge 1\,\right] \tag{4.2}\]

The evidence table is built over the four Alaska ecoregions only; v7c does not apply the rule elsewhere. A channel that does not reach an ecoregion is left out of the sum rather than counted as a zero. A channel that reaches it and finds nothing counts as a zero. The rule is a yes/no question over all channels together; the channels are reported separately below so that what each can and cannot see stays visible.

4.2.3 Rule 2: the right whale Gulf rule

\[ \text{drop}_{\textit{E. glacialis},\,e} = 1 \quad \text{for } e \in \{\text{EGOA},\ \text{WCGOA}\} \tag{4.3}\]

The evidence is of a different kind from rule 1: the species has a handful of Gulf records, all vagrants, against thousands in the Atlantic, and none of the independent range sources places it in the Gulf (see The right whale).

4.2.4 Applying a drop: critical habitat is never excluded

Both rules edit the merged model the same way (msens::zone_exclude_sql()):

\[ v'_{s,c} = \begin{cases} \text{absent} & \text{if } \text{drop}_{s,e(c)} = 1 \text{ and } c \notin H_s \\ v_{s,c} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \tag{4.4}\]

where \(v_{s,c}\) is the v7 merged value and \(H_s\) the cells the species holds under a designated critical-habitat dataset. A dropped cell is removed from the model, not set to zero, and every kept cell keeps its v7 value exactly. No v7 critical habitat for these species lies in an excluded ecoregion, so the guard is asserted by the build rather than exercised (n_ch_lost = 0 in build_v7c.json).

4.2.5 Rule 3: the coverage floor κ

Unchanged from v7b. A component (a species category, or primary productivity) is reportable for a Program Area only where its scored cells cover enough of the area:

\[ \text{coverage}_{z,g} = \frac{\sum_{c \in z,\ \text{scored}} w_c}{\sum_{c \in z} w_c} \,, \qquad \text{reportable}_{z,g} \iff \text{coverage}_{z,g} \ge \kappa \tag{4.5}\]

with \(w_c\) the share of cell \(c\) inside Program Area \(z\) and \(\kappa\) = 5 %. Below the floor the component is not reportable: it is left out of the composite, never published as a zero.

4.2.6 Why a drop, and not a weight

An alternative was worked through first: keep a turtle where it has been recorded rarely, but down-weight it by how rarely. The weight is the encounter rate per unit of looking, relative to a reference coast (California, Oregon and Washington), divided by what the distribution model already says about the same contrast (the encounter-rate notebook, Section 4.3.6):

\[ w_{s,e} = \min\left(1,\; \frac{\lambda_{s,e} / \lambda_{s,\text{ref}}}{\bar S_{s,e} / \bar S_{s,\text{ref}}}\right) \tag{4.6}\]

where \(\lambda\) is turtles found per unit of looking and \(\bar S\) is mean modeled suitability per cell. v7c does not use it, because a weight cannot reach the score:

  • The ecoregional rescale cancels it. Each component is rescaled to 0–100 within its ecoregion (Equation 10.4). A weight \(w\) shared by every turtle cell of an ecoregion scales the cell scores and both endpoints alike, so it divides out, apart from rounding to whole numbers (next point): \[ \frac{w\,x_c - w\,x_{\min}}{w\,x_{\max} - w\,x_{\min}} = \frac{x_c - x_{\min}}{x_{\max} - x_{\min}} \tag{4.7}\] A weight that differs between species changes only the mix of species within the ecoregion.
  • The integer floor keeps every cell. The scoring database stores merged values as integers floored at 1, \(v' = \max(1, \mathrm{round}(w\,v))\). Even a weight near 0 (the posterior never reaches exactly 0 for a species with no records) leaves the species in every cell at a value of 1. That is a flat surface the rescale treats like any other, and it still counts in the composite.

A weight can therefore at most reshuffle species within an ecoregion; taking a species out of an ecoregion’s score takes a drop. Where a species has been recorded, v7c keeps it at its v7 value. In the Gulf of Alaska the two fair comparisons disagree: the strandings line puts the weight at 1 for all four recorded species, while the iNaturalist line, which covers only the years since about 2008, would down-weight them strongly. The Alaska Region’s records since 2003 (request 1 below) would settle which holds today.

4.3.5 The right whale

In v7 the North Atlantic right whale’s Gulf of America range comes from two inputs only: the IUCN range polygon, valued at a flat 50, and AquaMaps. Every other line of evidence stops at the Atlantic. Designated critical habitat lies entirely in the Atlantic. The OBIS distribution-model ensemble, at its 10th-percentile training-presence threshold, has no Gulf cell. The habitat-based density models of Roberts et al. (2016), fitted to line-transect surveys of both the Atlantic and the Gulf, model the species in the Atlantic only. The Gulf records are a handful against thousands in the Atlantic, channel by channel. They are vagrants: NOAA’s 2006 release names the confirmed Gulf sightings of the time — 1963 off Longboat Key, 2004 off Panama City, and a mother and calf first seen off Corpus Christi in January 2006 (NOAA Fisheries Service Southeast Regional Office and Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission 2006; Ward-Geiger et al. 2011).

Three things move the composite, and nothing else does:

  • Aleutian Arc and St. George Basin lose their turtle component. Both lie mostly in the East Bering Sea. The record rule removes all but a sliver of their turtle cells: the remaining cells belong by majority to the Gulf of Alaska ecoregion. κ then removes that sliver as not reportable (table below). The two rules together empty both Program Areas of turtles. With the record rule alone, the Aleutian Arc would keep a turtle component near 0 and average it into the composite (the record rule only column). St. George Basin’s turtle sliver (1.5 % of the area in v7) was already below κ before the record rule touched it, so its composite change (33.9 → 38.6) is κ’s alone, exactly as in v7b; it rises because the component it loses scored below its average. Dropping a component cannot raise any cell’s sensitivity, but it changes which components the mean is taken over.
  • The Gulf of America Program Areas gain on mammals. Removing the right whale from both Gulf ecoregions moves those ecoregions’ marine-mammal minimum and maximum as well as the cell values, so the rescaled mammal component of GAA and GAB rises (second table below), and the composite with it.
  • Every other Program Area is unchanged. Gulf of Alaska, Kodiak, Cook Inlet and Shumagin keep all four recorded turtle species at their v7 values, so their composites are v7’s.

4.6 Compared with v7b

v7b answered the same question with a different rule: AquaMaps suitability entered the turtle merge only where it was at least 0.5, and a range cell with none was dropped. In the Bering Sea the two rules agree: Aleutian Arc and St. George Basin carry no turtles in either. They part in the Gulf of Alaska. The threshold left only the leatherback there, at suitability 0.50–0.58 off Southeast Alaska, and removed loggerhead, green and olive ridley from the Gulf of Alaska, where all three have been recorded. It also emptied Kodiak, Cook Inlet and Shumagin of turtles. v7c keeps all four recorded species in the Gulf of Alaska Program Areas at their v7 values (turtle component table above).

The threshold also reshaped turtle surfaces in the lower 48, raising the turtle component off California and lowering it in the Gulf of America. v7c leaves every turtle cell outside the dropped ecoregion exactly as v7 had it, so those components stay at v7’s values. v7b did not touch the right whale.

4.7 Caveats

  • Season is ignored. Green turtles are reported in Alaska mostly October–December and leatherbacks July–August. An annual rule suits an annual score; a seasonal product would need the same tables by month.
  • The channels cover different years. The NOAA Alaska file runs 1963–2020 and thins after 2002; iNaturalist starts around 2008; OBIS and GBIF pool more than a century, partly undated. A species absent from every channel in every year is the robust case the rule acts on. It is not a forecast: warming may bring turtles further north, and the rule should be re-run as records arrive.
  • The ecoregion is coarse. One record keeps a species across a whole ecoregion at its modeled value: the Gulf of Alaska keeps four species on a few dozen records (Alaska records table above).
  • A cell goes with its majority ecoregion. This is why the Aleutian Arc and St. George Basin keep a sliver of turtle cells under the record rule alone, which κ then removes.
  • Five data requests are open. Their answers could move the evidence, most of all the Alaska Region’s records since 2003:

4.8 Reproducing v7c

The exclusions are data: data/v7c/zone_exclusions.csv in the workflows repository, written by build_v7c_exclusions.qmd, applied by build_v7c.qmd through msens::cell_zone_major_sql() and msens::zone_exclude_sql(), each guarded by unit tests (a straddling cell goes with the larger side, a tie goes to the ecoregion key that sorts first, a critical-habitat cell survives, one taxon’s exclusion never touches another). The release records the rules it applied in its release_method table, including the exclusion table’s digest, and a build with the same flags on any machine must reproduce the committed reference digests (build_v7c.json).
