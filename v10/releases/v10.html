5  v10 — scoring across the nation

v10 is the first release to score across the nation. Every earlier release rescales each metric to 0–100 inside its ecoregion, so a score answers “where, within this region, is marine life most concentrated?” and cannot be compared between regions. v10 adds a second score beside it, the distribution share: each species’ value in a cell is divided by that species’ total over its whole modelled range, weighted by extinction risk, summed per category and zone, and expressed per square kilometre against the national average, which is 100. A zone at 100 holds exactly its fair share of a category’s importance for the area it covers; a zone at 300 holds three times that; a zone at 20, a fifth.

v10 changes the scoring and nothing upstream of it. It is built on the v8 build: the same datasets as public v7, ingested whole-range on the global 0.05° grid, with AquaX left out because its models are clipped to US waters (the whole-range denominator needs each species’ distribution outside the United States). No ingest and no merge is re-run. v8’s within-ecoregion metrics are kept unchanged in the same tables and serve as the control: the build digests them before and after it writes the share family, and stops if one differs.

ImportantServed on the review host only

v10 is a restricted pre-release. It is served only through the signed-in review host: the Atlas (Scores lens, Species lens) and this documentation. It is never what a reader gets by default, and promoting it is a separate decision.

The lineage has three steps, and they are different kinds of change:

  1. v7 is the public release. It scores the United States study area only, on a grid that has no cells beyond it.
  2. v8 is a data rebuild: the same datasets ingested whole-range on the global grid, with the merge and the scoring re-run. It was gated against v7 by the Program-Area equivalence check (msens::pra_score_delta) rather than assumed equivalent.
  3. v10 is a scoring change on v8’s data. It adds the share family and touches no ingest, no merge and no value in v8’s rows. One table gains rows: v8’s zone_taxon was stale for the subregion zone set (no Atlantic rows, and only part of the Pacific and USA rows), so v10 rebuilds it in full, from 115,700 to 138,728 rows; the rows v8 had hash identically, and no metric moves.

On the 20 Program Areas that v7 and v8 both score, v8’s within-ecoregion composite differs from v7’s by a mean of 4.1 points (largest 10.3), with a correlation of 0.937.

Read from the published tables: across the 17 metrics that v8 and v10 both publish for Program Areas, the largest absolute difference between them is 0. The build’s own gate requires the rows to be identical.

Numbering is not chronology. v9 (AquaX supersedes AquaMaps in US waters) was built before v10, but v10 does not descend from it: AquaX’s models are masked to US waters, so a species’ range outside the United States, which the share’s denominator needs, is not in them. v10’s parent is v8.

5.2 The method

The method has one idea and a few definitions. The full treatment, with the reasoning behind each choice, is in the Scoring chapter (Section 12.4); the definitions are repeated here so that this page can be read on its own.

The species share. For species \(s\), cell \(c\), merged value \(v_{s,c}\) (0–100, Chapter 11) and cell area \(A_c\):

\[ \text{share}_{s,c} = \frac{v_{s,c}\, A_c}{\sum_{c' \in \text{range}(s)} v_{s,c'}\, A_{c'}} \tag{5.1}\]

The denominator runs over the species’ whole modelled range, including land: the merged global surface carries a bird’s entire range, and the ocean grid holds only its marine cells, so a range cell that is not in the ocean grid takes the area of its latitude row. A species’ shares sum to 1 over its whole range.

The category share. Cells sum their species within a category \(g\), weighted by extinction risk:

\[ \text{share}_{c,g} = \frac{1}{100} \sum_{s \in g} w_{s,c}\, \text{share}_{s,c} \tag{5.2}\]

where \(w_{s,c}\) is the species’ governing er_score (Chapter 10). For the sea turtles, whose merged value already is extinction risk times suitability, \(w\) = 100 passes the value through. Because each species is divided by its own total, a turtle’s risk level cancels (its weight is 1 whatever its status) and only the spatial pattern of risk times suitability survives. Every turtle in v8 has the maximum score, so nothing moves.

The zone roll-up. A zone adds its cells, each weighted by the part of the cell inside the zone, \(p_{zc}\) (zone_cell.pct_covered ÷ 100). The area of cell \(c\) inside the zone is \(A_c\,p_{zc}\), so the roll-up is weighted by coverage times cell area:

\[ \text{share}_{z,g} = \sum_c \text{share}_{c,g}\, p_{zc} \,, \qquad \text{pct}^{\text{world}}_{z,g} = \frac{100\,\text{share}_{z,g}}{\sum_{s \in g} w_s / 100} \,, \qquad \text{pct}^{\text{nation}}_{z,g} = \frac{100\,\text{share}_{z,g}}{\text{share}_{\text{USA},g}} \tag{5.3}\]

The two percentages are the companions of the score: the share of the world’s, and of the nation’s, risk-weighted importance for the category that lies inside the zone. They are totals, so they grow with the zone’s area.

The score. It removes the area. With \(A_z = \sum_c A_c\,p_{zc}\):

\[ \text{v10}_{z,g} = 100 \; \frac{\text{share}_{z,g} \,/\, \text{share}_{\text{USA},g}}{A_z \,/\, A_{\text{USA}}} \tag{5.4}\]

It is the zone’s fraction of the nation’s importance for the category divided by its fraction of the nation’s surface, a location quotient (Isserman 1977). The national reference is the study area: every cell flagged in_usa, each in full, summed directly. It is not v8’s USA pseudo-zone, which is the union of the subregion polygons and omits coastal and estuarine study-area cells that hold scored species, so a species’ national share would not be its US total.

Reportability. A component is reported for a zone only if the cells that carry a value for it cover at least \(\kappa\) of the zone’s area (area-weighted):

\[ \text{cov}_{z,g} = \frac{\sum_{c \in z:\ \text{share}_{c,g}\ \text{exists}} p_{zc} A_c}{\sum_{c \in z} p_{zc} A_c} \ge \kappa \tag{5.5}\]

with \(\kappa\) = 5 % in v10. Below the floor the component is not reportable: it has no row, it is left out of the composite, and it is never published as a zero. A zero would lower the composite for a reason that has nothing to do with sensitivity. The floor is geometric (which cells carry a value, not the value itself) and applies to the across-the-nation family only; v8’s within-ecoregion rows are the control and carry none.

Primary productivity has no species. Its quotient is the zone’s mean productivity over the national mean, on the same scale, with the mean taken over all of a zone’s cells (a cell without a value counts as 0):

\[ \text{v10}_{z,\text{primprod}} = 100\;\frac{\bar{n}_z}{\bar{n}_{\text{USA}}} \tag{5.6}\]

The composite is the equal-weight mean of the components reportable in the zone, the species categories and productivity alike, so it also reads 100 for a zone that is nationally average across the board:

\[ \text{composite}_z = \frac{1}{\lvert R_z \rvert} \sum_{k \in R_z} \text{v10}_{z,k} \tag{5.7}\]

where \(R_z\) is the set of reportable components. Equal weights are a stated policy choice, the convention of the Ocean Health Index (Halpern et al. 2012; Samhouri et al. 2012); no richness-damping exponent is applied, and the number of species present and their mean share are published beside the score so a reader can tell an area that scores by richness from one that scores by dependence.

5.3 Three denominators, one flag

The headline divides each species by its whole range. Two other denominators are computed beside it, as columns of the same table, so that the dependence of the answer on this choice can be read rather than argued:

A sensitivity column treats the floored cells of the sea turtles. A turtle’s merged value is floored at 1 where risk × suitability rounds below 1, including every range cell with no suitability (the floor-of-1 range fill), which fills its range rather than describing habitat. The headline keeps those cells, so the surface stays continuous; the _strip column removes them from both sides of the share.

Where a species’ denominator comes from. The whole-range total needs a source for every scored species, taken in this order:

  1. a curated fallback fraction of the species’ range that lies in US waters (data/us_share_fallback.csv in the workflows repository), for species whose only models are national, such as range maps from FWS and NMFS: their “global” surface is their US range;
  2. the merged global surface, for every range-constrained species;
  3. for species modelled only by AquaMaps, the AquaMaps footprint outside the United States plus the merged surface inside.

A species with only national models and no curated fraction is left unresolved, and the build stops rather than score it at a share of 1.

In v10, of 17,108 scored species, 2,105 take the merged global surface, 14,991 the AquaMaps footprint outside the United States plus the merged surface inside, and 12 a curated fallback fraction (9 endemic, 3 literature); 0 are unresolved or take another source.

A build with the same flags on any machine must reproduce them; BUILD_REFERENCE=1 is the only way to move them. A render with other flags is a sensitivity run and is not compared.

The build refuses to publish the family unless a set of plausibility targets hold on the data. The Gulf of America Program Areas must stand far above the national average for turtles and the Aleutian Arc well below it; St. George Basin’s turtles must be not reportable; Kemp’s ridley must be the largest contributor to the turtle component of Gulf of America Program Area A; at least two of the four Arctic Program Areas (Beaufort Sea, Chukchi Sea, Hope Basin, Norton Basin) must keep a marine-mammal component (if they lose it, the denominator or its fallback is wrong and the build stops); and the Alaska Program Areas together must hold a small fraction of the nation’s turtle importance. A failed target stops the render rather than being listed in a table afterwards.

5.7 What v10 does not contain

v10 is one scoring change on a fixed set of inputs. Each of these belongs to the release after it, and none is in v10:

  • AquaX. Its models are clipped to US waters, so they cannot supply a whole-range denominator.
  • OBIS species-distribution models, and the density surfaces of the NOAA and NCCOS survey models.
  • Record-based exclusions. The turtle and right-whale records that v7c applied are not applied here.
  • The year-integrated productivity. v10 rescales nothing; its productivity quotient uses the productivity metric v8 already holds.
  • Changes to the sea turtles. Their merge is v8’s; moving them to the distinct-population path is a later change.
  • v9’s spatial extinction risk for the 19 NMFS DPS-listed species (the humpback, the Southern Resident killer whale, the salmonid ESUs and the others whose listed populations lie below species level). v10 inherits v8’s species-wide extinction risk for them.

5.8 Caveats

  • The denominator is a modelling choice. A species’ share is only as good as the range it is divided by. The merged global surface is the best-supported source; the AquaMaps footprint outside the United States is an environmental-envelope prediction; the curated fraction is a literature value. The source is recorded for every species, and the usa and pooled columns exist so that the choice can be tested.
  • The score is relative to the study area, not to the world. 100 is the national average concentration of the area the program scores. A zone can score 300 and still hold a small fraction of the world’s importance; read the percent of the world beside it.
  • Rankings change; levels change more. Two composites on different scales agree on order only as far as ρ says. Never put a within-ecoregion score and an across-the-nation score in one column.
  • A not-reportable component is absent, not low. Its row does not exist, and the composite averages the components that do.

5.9 Reproducing v10

The method is code before it is prose. The rules are msens::share_sql() (the scoring SQL), msens::denom_sql() (each species’ whole-range denominator) and msens::share_run() (the composition and its invariants), each guarded by unit tests with one synthetic fixture per rule (test-share.R): an endemic species scores the whole of its share in its zone, shares sum to 1 over a species’ range, two zones of different area with the same intensity score the same but differ in the percent of the world, a zone at the national average scores 100, a component just under the floor has no score and one just over has, the composite skips an absent component, and a national-only species with a curated fraction is divided by its US total over that fraction.

The workflow is two notebooks of the workflows repository, build_taxon_global_total.qmd (the denominators, written to taxon_denom) and score_share_metrics.qmd (the share family, the release methods and the gates), run after bootstrap_version has cloned v8 and verified the clone against v8’s checkpoints.
