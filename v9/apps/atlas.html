Appendix A — Atlas (beta) — the static, in-browser scores, species and places app

current

B Atlas

B.1 Overview

The Atlas (https://preview.marinesensitivity.org/v9/atlas/) is a third, newer way to explore the MST, currently labelled beta: one static web app, built with Svelte and MapLibre, that runs entirely in the browser. Where the Scores and Species Shiny apps are server-rendered R processes, the Atlas has no application server of its own: scores, flowers, tables and reports are computed in your browser, by a DuckDB instance compiled to WebAssembly (DuckDB-WASM) querying the release’s published Parquet directly over HTTPS. It still depends on a few other services for what a browser cannot compute itself — score and species map tiles come from the project’s titiler, zone outlines from PMTiles on S3, and the basemap from CARTO — but there is no Atlas-specific backend.

On the public host the release is a query parameter (?ver=v7), resolved client-side against the published versions.json and defaulting to the promoted release when absent. A restricted pre-release is meant to be served the same way the preview Scores and Species apps are, at https://preview.marinesensitivity.org/{ver}/atlas/ (the version as the URL path, since Cloudflare Access scopes its reviewer policies by path, never by query) — this route is planned, not yet live; see Known Limitations below.

Figure B.1 shows the default Scores view: the 0.05° cell grid colored by a composite sensitivity score across U.S. waters, dark theme (the app’s default), with the tool rail on the left, the Layers panel open by default, and the basemap’s attribution in the lower-left corner.

Desktop vs. phone layout. On a wide viewport the five rail tools open a single dockable panel that floats over the map; on a phone the identical five tools sit in a labelled bottom tab bar, and the panel becomes a bottom sheet with three heights (peek, half, full). Figure B.2 shows the phone layout, and Figure B.3 its top bar’s More (⋯) menu.

B.2 Quick start

Desktop:

  1. Open Places, and either choose a Program Area from its list or draw a Rectangle (click, move, click, then Done).
  2. Select the drawn place in the list to see its coverage note, flower and component table, and (on request) its species table (a Program Area’s row already shows its composite).
  3. Rename it inline if you like.
  4. Places › Share › Copy link — the fastest way to hand someone the same place.
  5. Places › Report for a printable, downloadable document (Print, HTML, ZIP or Word).

Phone: the tool rail is the bottom tab bar; use the Program Area chooser or Enter coordinates rather than drawing, and the file picker rather than dropping a file on the map (that gesture needs a desktop pointer).

B.3 Getting around

The top bar, left to right on desktop: the MST mark and title; a release chip that opens the version picker; a Scores | Species lens switch; a search field; then, right-aligned, Share, Report, a (?) Help menu (Take a tour, keyboard shortcuts, a Docs link), a Feedback button, an (i) About button, and a sun/moon theme toggle. On a phone (below 900 px) the title collapses to the mark, and Share, Report, Help, Feedback, About and the theme toggle fold into one More (⋯) button beside a search button; below 381 px the mark hides too. The keyboard-shortcut list is desktop-only.

The tool rail is five controls, always in the same order, on every viewport: Layers · Places · Flower plot · Table · Report. In the Species lens the Flower control fades in place — it has no meaning for a single species — but stays visible with a tooltip explaining why.

The panel that a rail tool opens is one dockable surface: dock left, right or bottom; resize by dragging its map-facing edge or with arrow keys; maximize to the whole stage; or collapse to a small labelled pill (clicking any rail tool re-opens it). Its dock/size/collapsed state is chrome, remembered per viewport, never written into the shareable URL. On a phone the same panel is the bottom sheet, with peek/half/full detents. On a desktop the map’s legend floats in a corner clear of the panel, on whichever side it is docked, and hides while the panel is maximized; on a phone it is a compact Legend chip above the sheet that opens the full legend, moving into the sheet’s own header at full height.

B.4 Scores lens

The Scores lens shows the composite, extinction-risk-weighted score for every 0.05° cell (or, aggregated, every Program Area) of U.S. waters. Opening Layers (Figure B.4) expands the Data row into this lens’s own controls — study area, spatial unit, the score layer, color palette, a Sphere switch (globe vs. flat projection), and a Cells outside Program Areas switch where the release publishes that overlay — above the rest of the layer stack, listed top to bottom: Selection (pinned at the top), Zone outlines, Data, Place labels, Roads & buildings, Boundaries, Bathymetry (present but disabled until a relief layer is published) and Land & water at the bottom. Every row’s visibility and opacity can be changed, and most can be reordered — Data can never move above Zone outlines, so an opaque score raster can never hide the boundaries it is measured against — and Reset layers restores the default stack.

Clicking a cell or a Program Area selects it: the URL gains a sel=cell:<id> or sel=zone:<unit>:<key> token, which the Flower plot and Table tools then describe (Section B.8). A click on land or outside the scored area shows “No scored cell here” with its coordinates and leaves the selection unchanged.

B.5 Species lens

Switching to Species turns the top-bar search into a species picker (common or scientific name, with an “Only species in US waters” checkbox), and replaces the Data row’s contents inside Layers with the species card: the merged model as a filled pill marked ✓ with a gold edge, plus one pill per contributing input dataset marked ▶ with a grey edge. An input this release published no surface for renders as a struck-through, non-interactive pill — never simply omitted. Where the selected input publishes both, one representation toggle appears (“Original” vs. “Interpolated,” or “Delivered” vs. “As ingested” for a dataset already on the scoring grid). Selecting a species flies the map to that model’s extent; clicking it then returns that cell’s modelled value, or “No scored cell here” outside the model’s range. The legend floats in a corner of the map (a legend chip on a phone), titled with the species’ scientific name and its 1–100 range.

B.6 Places

Places is the tool the two Shiny apps do not have: any area you outline gets the same scores and species results the built-in Program Areas get. The empty state reads exactly: “No places yet. Choose a Program Area above, turn on pick mode and click one on the map, draw a shape, enter coordinates, or drop a file on the map.”

  • Pick a Program Area. Turning on pick mode and clicking only highlights it — Ctrl/Cmd-click, Shift-click or long-press to highlight several — and “Add to places (n)” is the button that actually adds it; several picks become one place. Or use the panel’s own Program Area chooser, which needs no pointer and works on a phone. Program Areas are shown by their bare key (e.g. GEO) today; a release that publishes names shows “St. George Basin (GEO)” instead.
  • Draw a Polygon, Rectangle or Circle: click, move, click for each vertex or corner (not a drag), then press Done — a polygon also closes when you click its first vertex again, or press Enter. A circle is stored as a 64-sided polygon. Dragging a finished shape’s corner does not edit it (see Known limitations).
  • Enter coordinates — the keyboard/screen-reader alternative to drawing, in a modal dialog: a bounding box (xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax), a list of lon, lat vertices one per line, or pasted WKT/GeoJSON. A refusal is shown verbatim — what went wrong, why, and how to fix it.
  • Upload a file, through the picker or by dropping it anywhere on the map: “GeoJSON, zipped shapefile, KML, GPX, FlatGeobuf, WKT or GeoPackage (not yet), up to 10 MB.” GeoPackage cannot actually be read yet — every .gpkg refuses immediately, reading “GeoPackage is not supported yet”; export the layer as GeoJSON or a zipped shapefile instead. Other rules that cause a refusal: polygons only (points and lines are refused, never buffered); a shapefile needs its .prj (projected coordinates are refused); at most 50,000 vertices; a zipped shapefile at most 50 MB uncompressed and 200 entries; and the shape must touch the U.S. study area. A file with several features becomes one place per feature (up to 20, named after the file and numbered) or one merged place, your choice.

Each row shows its area and, as soon as it is analysed, its data coverage percentage and composite score directly in the row — for a drawn, typed or uploaded place the same analysis runs for every such place in the list, not only the one currently selected; a Program Area row shows its own published composite the same way (e.g. “ALA … 28.3 composite”). Row actions: an inline rename (≤ 60 characters, drawn/uploaded places only), zoom to, duplicate (drawn/uploaded only), delete, and Open in report. Selecting a drawn, typed or uploaded place additionally expands its results panel below the list — a Program Area place does not get one here: to see its flower and species, click it on the map (spatial unit: Program Areas) or choose it in Table › Zones instead (Section B.8). The results panel shows a coverage note (“N% of this place is inside the US study area (a of b cells)”, or “This place is outside US waters here: no scores can be computed”), a flower plot, a component table, and — on request, with a “Loading species needs N tiles, about X MB. Continue?” prompt — a species table. Show analysis cells paints the analysed 0.05° cells (up to 20,000 of them). The list caps at 20 places (“n / 20 places”); Recent (this browser only) offers to add a removed place back. The footer holds Share, Download places (GeoJSON) and Report, each disabled with zero places.

Two different Share buttons. The top-bar Share simply copies the current address bar to the clipboard — no dialog, no summary, no length check. Places › Share opens the real dialog described next, and is the one to use whenever places are involved.

B.7 How places are stored in the link

A place never lives on a server: it is encoded directly into the URL fragment (#pl=, never the query string, so it is never sent to a server or a referrer), so opening a shared link reproduces the sender’s exact area. Every analysis runs on the decoded geometry, never the geometry as drawn, so the numbers can never diverge between sender and recipient. Places are joined with ~:

#pl=z.pa.GAA~g1.Gulf%20box.EAMBAQSf_grYrQPQDwAA0A_PDwA

(the decoded form; the address bar itself percent-encodes ~ and other reserved characters)

  • z.<set>.<key>[,<key>...] — a reference to a built-in zone (pa Program Area, pl Planning Area, er ecoregion, sr subregion); its geometry comes from the release’s own PMTiles.
  • g1.<name>.<base64url bytes> — a drawn, typed or uploaded polygon, carried inline as a compact binary encoding (see “For developers” below).
  • u.<name>.<8-hex-digit digest> — the fallback for a place too large to carry (see below): only a name and a digest travel.
NoteFor developers: the g1 byte layout

Each ring is quantized to a fixed precision (3 decimal digits, ~110 m — 4 digits when the place’s bounding box is under 0.5° in both directions), delta-encoded vertex-to-vertex as zigzag varints running across rings and polygons, and base64url-encoded with no general-purpose compression (delta varints are already dense). Longitudes are stored unwrapped, so a place crossing the antimeridian round-trips without a special case. An R decoder in msens is in preparation; the shared test fixture (tests/fixtures/place_codec.json) is written to match it byte for byte.

The link’s length is checked only when you open Places › Share. Adding a place, or copying the top-bar Share link, carries the unsimplified geometry (already quantized to 3–4 decimals) regardless of length. In that dialog: up to 2,000 characters silently; between 2,000 and 8,000 a note reads “This link is over 2,000 characters — some chat apps and older browsers truncate very long links,” with Copy anyway; only above 8,000 does the Douglas-Peucker ladder run — 0.001° doubling to 0.016°, a rung accepted only while the area changes by no more than 1% and every ring stays simple — aiming to bring the link back under 2,000 characters; it stops at the first rung that does, or, if none does, uses the coarsest accepted rung provided that still fits under 8,000. This is never silent: the dialog shows the before/after (vertices, area change, cells, Figure B.9), and you click “Simplify and update the numbers” to accept it — which is also what rewrites #pl=, so the copied link and the displayed numbers can never disagree. If even the coarsest rung is still too long, Copy link anyway copies every drawn, typed or uploaded place (zone places are unaffected) as a u. token instead — your own address bar and session keep the real shapes; the recipient of that copied link sees a named row with no results and a “not analysed yet” chip, never a prompt. The dialog’s own advice at that point is to download the place as GeoJSON and send that alongside the link. A single small rectangle almost always fits in the silent budget; that is not guaranteed for several places together, or a long base URL.

The rest of the view travels the same URL-is-the-state way, in the query string: ver, lens, lyr/sp, unit, area, map (camera), pal, sel, layers (the stack deltas from Section B.4), theme and tour — each written only when it differs from its default.

B.8 Flower plot and Table

Flower plot and Table are Scores-lens tools for a clicked cell or Program Area; in the Species lens, Table is a placeholder, and a drawn Place’s flower and species table live in the Places results panel instead (Section B.6), not here.

The flower draws one equal-width annular petal per score component, scaled to its rescaled score; the hub shows the plain mean of the components present (not a weighted mean). Since 0.10.41 the values also list in a real table (swatch, component, score, and a Mean row) below the plot.

Table opens three tabs: a sortable species table (Category, Taxon, Scientific name, Common name, ER code, ER score, Model, MMPA, MBTA, Area (km²), Avg. suitability, % of category — ESA and IUCN status are folded into ER code), a zones table (every Program Area ranked by the current layer, with “report on selected” once some are ticked), and a composition treemap (one box per species category, sized by suitability × extinction risk × area). Only the species table has the phone’s compact default columns and a Columns control; both tables get sticky headers/first column and horizontal scrolling that never spills the panel. Only the species table’s Download CSV exports it; the zones table and the composition treemap have no export yet. A drawn place’s Components and Species tables in the Places results panel each have their own working Export CSV.

B.9 Report

Report reports every place currently in the link (#pl=); with no places, it reports the single selected Program Area; with neither, it shows a chooser. A row’s own Open in report reports just that row’s place. The document renders progressively as its data arrives:

  • a header band — mark, release chip, generation time, permalink and QR code (a restricted release also gets a PREVIEW banner and print watermark);
  • an introduction;
  • a collapsible Parameters section (kind, keys or vertices, area, cell count, study-area share, token);
  • a map — interactive on screen; a static snapshot only in print and the HTML export;
  • one flower plot per place, under “Plot of Scores”;
  • a Table of Scores — component and overall scores, with a footnote when a component covers under 99% of the area, or is missing entirely because the release’s own 5% coverage floor dropped it;
  • a Summary of Species per place (a category × count cross-tab, plus the top 20 by score);
  • Sources and Method, with the release’s own citations — each query behind these numbers is a parity-tested twin (npm run parity, max|Δ| < 1e-9) of an msens R function, though “Reproduce in R” itself is not yet usable: it prints calls to msens::place_decode(), cells_in_polygon_grid() and scores_for_cells() that exist only on an unreleased msens branch;
  • a Provenance section (release, status/access, app build SHA, DuckDB-WASM version, tables read, SQL run).

Four exports, all built client-side: Print (the browser’s dialog; roughly five Letter pages); Download HTML (one self-contained file — the flowers stay inline SVG, only the map is an embedded PNG); a data package (ZIP) — README.md, CITATION.md, provenance.json, places.geojson (drawn/uploaded places only, not zone places), a scores_*.csv/species_*.csv pair per place, and query/*.sql; and a Word document (.docx) — the same tables as coded Word styles, plus the map and a rasterized flower per place.

B.10 Accessibility and performance notes

The Atlas ships a written WCAG 2.1 conformance note and a performance budget report in its own repository. As of the note’s own 0.10.24 (2026-09-23) evaluation the claim is “partially supports,” not full support, and it states plainly that no screen reader was actually driven in producing it — automated and manual keyboard testing stand in for that. Two skip links sit at the very top of the page: “Skip to the tools” and “Skip to the details panel.” The critical-path JavaScript was measured (0.10.20, 2026-09-23) at ~417 KB gzip against a 450 KB budget, plus ~140 KB for the MapLibre worker against 150 KB; a separate gate asserted the species lens paints its first data pixel within 2.5 s on a laptop and 4 s on CI. Both budgets may have moved since; see the linked reports for current numbers.

B.11 Feedback and versions

Feedback opens a dialog: a kind (bug / idea / question / data), a title, a description, an optional email, an annotatable screenshot, and a privacy checkbox (off by default) for including the current view’s link. On the live app, “Send” is not configured — no feedback endpoint is set in production — so the dialog says so and points to “Open as GitHub issue” instead, a separate button that opens a public issue in MarineSensitivity/atlas, labelled by kind, with the screenshot copied to your clipboard to paste in. Tell an analyst plainly: text sent this way is public. A restricted release has no GitHub route at all — “feedback cannot be sent from here right now.” Once an endpoint is configured, a public-release submission without an email is also filed as a public GitHub issue.

Versions follow the same public/restricted split as the rest of the MST (see Chapter 2): a public release opens directly on the public host; a restricted pre-release’s Atlas is meant to be reached only through the signed-in preview host — but that route is not yet deployed (see Known Limitations). Requesting a release the current host cannot show opens the version picker automatically: a restricted one offers “Continue on the preview host,” an unrecognized one reads “is not a version this host recognizes,” and either way the promoted release loads underneath while the picker explains why. In the picker, every public release other than the current one offers Switch.

B.12 Known limitations (as of 0.10.48)

  • The top-bar search box does nothing in the Scores lens; it only searches species, in the Species lens.
  • GeoPackage upload is listed as an accepted format (“GeoPackage (not yet)” in the drop zone’s own text) but cannot actually be read yet.
  • For v7, every one of a species’ input datasets shows struck through in the Species card — a release-side gap in what v7 publishes, not the app’s design; the Species app can still draw them.
  • Program Areas are shown by their bare key; “Name (KEY)” needs a release that publishes names.
  • Selecting a Program Area place opens no results panel in Places (its row does show a real composite score, but there is no coverage note or flower there) — click it on the map or choose it in Table › Zones to see its flower and species instead.
  • Dragging a drawn shape’s corner adds a new place rather than editing the original: delete the extra row, or redraw.
  • Feedback Send is not configured on the live app; use “Open as GitHub issue” (public).
  • The preview-host Atlas (/{ver}/atlas/) is not yet served; “Continue on the preview host” currently leads to a sign-in and then the landing page, not the Atlas.
  • The report’s “Reproduce in R” snippets call msens functions that exist only on an unreleased branch.

B.13 Generating Screenshots

The atlas repository’s own scripts/eyes-shots.mjs captures the standard states most of this chapter’s figures come from (18 states, phone 390×844 at 2x pixel density and desktop 1280×800, dark theme), run against a live or locally built app:

ATLAS_URL=https://marinesensitivity.org/atlas OUT=.tmp/eyes node scripts/eyes-shots.mjs

A few figures here (the Share dialog and its simplification ladder, the Coordinate dialog’s refusal state, the version picker, and one Report scroll position) are not among that script’s own states; they were captured by hand from the same live build, driving the app to the specific state each caption describes.
