12  Scoring

Scoring turns merged species distributions and extinction-risk weights into per-cell sensitivity metrics, which are then rescaled within ecoregions and aggregated to management zones.

TipInteractive walkthrough

The Scoring Walkthrough animates this pipeline — input rasters, merge, ecoregional rescaling and component averaging, step by step.

12.1 Cell-level scoring

For each cell, sensitivity is the extinction-risk-weighted sum of species presence within a species category:

\[ \text{score}_{c,g} = \sum_{s=1}^{S_g} \frac{\text{er\_score}_s \times v_{s,c}}{100} \tag{12.1}\]

where \(\text{score}_{c,g}\) is the score for cell \(c\) and category \(g\); \(\text{er\_score}_s\) is species \(s\)’s extinction-risk weight (1–100, Chapter 10); \(v_{s,c}\) is the merged model value for that species in that cell (0–100, Chapter 11); and \(S_g\) is the number of valid species in the category. Dividing by 100 returns the product to a 0–100 scale.

In plain terms: for every cell the MST adds up the contributions of the species found there. A cell holding many species that are both likely present and at high risk scores higher — which is how places where rare or threatened species concentrate are identified.

12.1.1 Two exceptions: species whose risk varies across the range

For sea turtles, extinction risk is already inside the merged values via the multiplicative merge (Chapter 11), so the formula collapses to a plain sum:

\[ \text{score}_{c,g} = \sum_{s=1}^{S_g} v_{merged,s,c} \tag{12.2}\]

er_score = 100 acts as a pass-through multiplier that cancels against the division. Cells inside an Endangered DPS therefore contribute more than cells inside a Threatened one, for the same species at the same suitability.

For the NMFS DPS-listed species (the humpback whale, Southern Resident killer whale, salmonid ESUs, …) the merged value is the suitability alone and the risk is a per-cell weight carried beside it, so the species-level er_score is replaced by the cell’s:

\[ \text{score}_{c,g} = \frac{1}{100} \sum_{s=1}^{S_g} er_{s,c} \times v_{s,c} \tag{12.3}\]

Numerically this is the turtle product taken one step later; the difference is that the merged model these species publish is their distribution, not their risk-weighted distribution. Each taxon’s rule is recorded as er_mode in the scoring taxon table (taxon — the scalar weight; premultiplied — sea turtles; cell — DPS-listed species).

12.2 Metrics

One extinction-risk metric exists per scored species category — invertebrate, fish, coral, primary_producer, bird, mammal, turtle — plus primary productivity, which carries no extinction-risk weighting.

12.3 Ecoregional rescaling

Raw cell scores vary naturally between ecoregions, through differences in species richness, oceanography and biogeography. Each metric is therefore rescaled to 0–100 within each BOEM Ecoregion:

\[ \text{score}'_{c} = \frac{\text{score}_{c} - \text{score}_{min}}{\text{score}_{max} - \text{score}_{min}} \times 100 \tag{12.4}\]

with the minimum and maximum taken over that metric’s cells within the ecoregion.

ImportantScores are relative within an ecoregion, never absolute

A score of 100 marks the most sensitive cell for that metric within that ecoregion — not globally. A 30 in Alaska and a 30 in the Pacific describe different ecological contexts.

Scores are therefore directly comparable among Program Areas within one ecoregion, and should not be compared across ecoregions.

Why rescale at all? Without it, naturally species-rich areas would almost always score highest simply for holding more species. More species may well indicate greater ecological importance — but it can equally be argued that the fewer species an ecosystem has, the more each one matters to its function and resilience. Rescaling within ecoregions keeps scores from being dominated by richness. Comparing across ecoregions would require settling that argument with a mathematical relationship between the two views, which remains a live academic debate.

12.4 Across the nation: the distribution share

ImportantTwo scores, two questions

The ecoregionally rescaled score (Equation 12.4) is relative within an ecoregion: a 100 marks the most sensitive cell for that metric within that ecoregion, so it is comparable among the Program Areas of one ecoregion and answers “where, within this region, is marine life most concentrated?”.

The distribution share, published from v10, is comparable across the whole study area. It is the fraction of each species’ range that a cell or a zone holds, weighted by extinction risk and summed, so it answers “how much of the nation’s importance for this category is here, per square kilometre, compared with the national average?”. National tables use the share; within-region maps and rankings use the rescaled score. Never mix the two in one column or one composite.

The rescaled scores above cannot be compared across ecoregions, because every ecoregion contains a 100 by construction. For reporting across regions, a release that carries the share family computes a second score from the same merged models, in which every species is first normalized to itself. The within-ecoregion metrics are kept unchanged beside it, as the control.

12.4.1 The species share and the category share

A species’ share of a cell is the part of its whole modelled range that the cell holds, where the range is measured as suitability times area:

\[ \text{share}_{s,c} = \frac{v_{s,c}\, A_c}{\sum_{c' \in \text{range}(s)} v_{s,c'}\, A_{c'}} \tag{12.5}\]

where \(v_{s,c}\) is the merged value of species \(s\) in cell \(c\) (Chapter 11) and \(A_c\) is the area of the cell. The shares of one species sum to 1 over its whole range (inside the nation they sum to the species’ US fraction), and the build asserts it. The denominator runs over the species’ whole modelled range, land included, because the merged global surfaces keep a bird’s entire range; a cell that is not in the ocean grid takes the area of its latitude row. Where a species has no merged global surface, the denominator comes from a second source (see Three denominators below).

Cells then sum their species within a category, weighted by extinction risk as in Equation 12.1:

\[ \text{share}_{c,g} = \frac{1}{100} \sum_{s \in g} w_{s,c}\, \text{share}_{s,c} \tag{12.6}\]

where \(w_{s,c}\) is the governing extinction-risk score (er_score, Chapter 10) for most species, and 100 for sea turtles, whose merged value already carries their risk (Equation 12.2). Because the share divides each species by its own total, the risk level of such a turtle cancels: its weight is 1 whatever its status, and only the spatial pattern of risk times suitability survives. The scored categories are invertebrate, fish, coral, primary_producer, bird, mammal, turtle, and primary productivity enters the composite separately, as its own quotient (below).

12.4.2 From cells to zones

A zone adds up its cells, each weighted by the part of the cell that lies inside the zone:

\[ \text{share}_{z,g} = \sum_{c} \text{share}_{c,g}\, p_{zc} \,, \qquad \text{pct}_{z,g} = \frac{100\; \text{share}_{z,g}}{\sum_{s \in g} w_s / 100} \,, \qquad \text{pct}^{\text{nation}}_{z,g} = \frac{100\; \text{share}_{z,g}}{\text{share}_{\text{USA},g}} \tag{12.7}\]

where \(p_{zc}\) is the coverage of cell \(c\) by zone \(z\) (zone_cell.pct_covered ÷ 100). Cell area enters twice: in the numerator of the species share (Equation 12.5), and through \(p_{zc}\) as the area of the cell that is inside the zone, \(A_c\,p_{zc}\). This is the v10 rule, and it differs from how the ecoregionally rescaled scores are aggregated, which weights by coverage alone (Section 12.7). \(\text{pct}_{z,g}\) is the percent of the world’s risk-weighted importance for the category that lies inside the zone, the amount at stake; \(\text{pct}^{\text{nation}}_{z,g}\) is the percent of the nation’s. Both are totals and grow with the zone’s area.

The score removes the area:

\[ \text{v10}_{z,g} = 100 \; \frac{\text{share}_{z,g} \,/\, \text{share}_{\text{USA},g}}{A_z \,/\, A_{\text{USA}}} \tag{12.8}\]

with \(A_z = \sum_c A_c\, p_{zc}\), and \(\text{share}_{\text{USA},g}\) and \(A_{\text{USA}}\) the same sums over the national reference. The reference is the study area: every cell flagged in_usa, each in full. It is not the USA pseudo-zone, which is the union of the subregion polygons and omits coastal and estuarine study-area cells that carry scored species.

The score is the zone’s fraction of the nation’s importance for the category divided by its fraction of the nation’s surface. It is a location quotient (Isserman 1977): 100 means the zone holds exactly its fair share of the component per square kilometre, 300 three times the national concentration, 20 a fifth of it. The reference point is therefore identical for every zone and every component and does not depend on any other zone’s score; the national reference is recomputed from each release’s own data, so 100 means the national average of that release. In the reference-point typology of the Ocean Health Index it is a distance to a reference rather than a comparison with the best performer (Halpern et al. 2012; Samhouri et al. 2012; OECD and JRC 2008).

Because the zone total sums over species, it equals the number of species present times their mean risk-weighted fraction: richness still counts, once per species rather than once per cell, and both factors are published beside the score (_n_species, _mean_share_*) so that a reader can tell an area that scores by richness from one that scores by dependence. No richness-damping exponent is applied.

In the language of conservation planning the zone total is rarity-weighted richness (Williams et al. 1996; Albuquerque and Beier 2015) and weighted endemism (Crisp et al. 2001) computed on a suitability surface with a risk weight, and it is the per-feature normalization Zonation applies to every input layer (Moilanen et al. 2005). It rewards irreplaceability (Kukkala and Moilanen 2013): a zone holding an entire endemic population scores at the top, a zone holding a few vagrants near zero, and no species-rich region can dominate merely by count. It is blind to the dense habitat of wide-ranging species, which is what the ecoregional score continues to show. Its denominator is a modelling choice whose reference area is stated, as the national-responsibility literature requires (Schmeller et al. 2008, 2014), and it follows the global-population thresholds of site designation (IUCN 2016; Ramsar Convention 2005).

12.4.3 Primary productivity and the composite

Primary productivity has no species. Its quotient is the zone’s mean productivity over the national mean, on the same scale:

\[ \text{v10}_{z,\text{primprod}} = 100 \; \frac{\bar{n}_z}{\bar{n}_{\text{USA}}} \tag{12.9}\]

where \(\bar{n}_z\) is the area-weighted mean of net primary productivity over all of the zone’s cells (a cell without a value counts as 0, as the published pct_area down-weighting does for the rescaled score) and \(\bar{n}_{\text{USA}}\) is the same estimator over the national reference.

The composite is the equal-weight mean of the components that are reportable in the zone, each on the same scale, so it too reads 100 for a zone that is nationally average across the board. A component that is not reportable (next subsection) is left out of the mean, exactly as a category with no cells in the zone is. Equal weights are a stated policy choice, the convention of the Ocean Health Index (Halpern et al. 2012). The release chapter reports, per category, the score and the percent of the world’s importance at stake, and, for the composite only, the rank change against the ecoregional ranking, with the Spearman rank correlation between the two composite rankings in that table’s caption (OECD and JRC 2008).

12.4.4 The reporting floor

A component is reported for a zone only if its scored cells cover at least \(\kappa\) of the zone’s area, where coverage is the area-weighted fraction of the zone’s cells that carry a value for the category:

\[ \text{cov}_{z,g} = \frac{\sum_{c \in z:\ \text{share}_{c,g}\ \text{exists}} p_{zc} A_c}{\sum_{c \in z} p_{zc} A_c} \;\ge\; \kappa \tag{12.10}\]

with \(\kappa\) = 5 % in v11. Below the floor the component is not reportable, and it has no score row: the tables print n/r and the composite leaves it out. It is never set to zero, which would lower the composite for a reason unrelated to sensitivity. The floor is defined on the footprint, not on the value. It applies to the across-the-nation family only: the within-ecoregion rows of the same release are the control and carry no floor.

In v11 the floor removes 0 Program Area × component pairs of the 149 that have scored cells.

12.4.5 Three denominators, one flag

The headline divides each species by its whole modelled range (global). Two other denominators are computed beside it, as columns of the same table, so that a reader can see how much the choice matters:

Each species’ denominator comes from the first source that applies: a curated fallback fraction of the world’s range that lies in US waters (for species whose only models are national, such as FWS and NMFS range maps); the merged global surface; and, for a species with neither, the AquaMaps footprint outside the US plus the merged surface inside. The source of every species is recorded in the taxon_denom table.

In v11, of 17,115 scored species, 2,111 take the merged global surface, 14,992 the AquaMaps footprint outside the US plus the merged surface inside, and 12 a curated fallback fraction (9 endemic, 3 literature); 0 are unresolved.

A separate sensitivity column treats the floored cells of the sea turtles. A turtle’s merged value is floored at 1 where risk × suitability rounds below 1, including every range cell with no suitability (the floor-of-1 range fill), which fills the range rather than describing habitat. The headline keeps those cells, so the surface stays continuous; the _strip column removes them from both sides of the share.

12.4.6 What is published

Check on v11. Under the global denominator, the share of the nation’s risk-weighted sea-turtle importance that lies in each region’s Program Areas is Alaska 0.0 %, Gulf of America 21.2 %, Pacific 0.6 %.

12.5 Spatial units

Geometry is versioned by its own vintage rather than by release, so several releases legitimately share one boundary set — one Program Area geometry covers v2 through v8, and one ecoregion geometry covers every release. Each release’s manifest names the vintage it used, which is what lets this book draw the correct outlines for the version it documents instead of the newest ones.

12.5.1 Geographic scope

The analysis covers 20 BOEM Program Areas from the 11th National Draft Proposed Program (2025), spanning Alaska, the Pacific and the Gulf of America. The Atlantic OCS is not part of this program cycle, so ecoregions containing no Program Area are outside the scored footprint.

The Gulf of America Program Areas (GAA and GAB) each span two ecoregions — Western and Central Gulf of America, and Eastern Gulf of America. Because rescaling happens before aggregation, cells are rescaled against their own ecoregion’s range, and the Program Area score is then the coverage-weighted mean across all its cells regardless of ecoregion (Section 12.7). Local ecological context is preserved even where a Program Area crosses a boundary.

12.6 Primary productivity

Net primary productivity from the VGPM satellite model enters as its own metric (primprod), without extinction-risk weighting. The raw values (metric tons C km-2 yr-1) are rescaled within each ecoregion on the same 0–100 basis as everything else.

12.7 Zone aggregation

Rescaled cell scores are aggregated to each spatial unit by a mean weighted by the share of each cell that lies inside the unit:

\[ \text{score}_{zone} = \frac{\sum_{c \in zone} \text{score}'_c \, p_{c,zone}}{\sum_{c \in zone} p_{c,zone}} \tag{12.11}\]

where \(p_{c,zone}\) is the coverage of cell \(c\) by the zone (zone_cell.pct_covered, 100 for a cell wholly inside it and a fraction along its boundary). Cell area is not a weight in this rule. A cell on a 0.05° grid is smaller at high latitude, and the weights here do not correct for it: each cell counts by the part of it inside the zone, not by its area. This is the rule the scoring notebooks of v7 and later compute. The component’s zone score is then multiplied by the fraction of the zone’s covered cells that carry a value for that component (pct_area), so a component present on only a sliver of a zone cannot weigh as much as one that covers it.

From v11, the across-the-nation family (Section 12.4) does weight by area: a cell enters a zone with its own area times the part of it inside the zone, \(A_c\,p_{c,zone}\), and the national reference sums the same areas. The within-ecoregion rows of the same release keep the coverage weighting above, unchanged.

12.8 Visualization

12.8.1 Flower plot

The flower plot summarizes a zone’s sensitivity profile by category, after the Ocean Health Index (Halpern et al. 2012):

  • petal length — the rescaled score (0–100) for that category; longer means more sensitive
  • petal width — that category’s weight in the composite; wider means more influence
  • centre — the weighted mean across categories

It lets a reader see at a glance which ecological elements drive sensitivity in an area. The scores app implements an interactive version.

12.8.2 Treemap

The treemap shows how individual species contribute to a score, nested first by extinction-risk category and then by taxonomy, with zoom down to single species.

12.8.3 Score rasters

Cell-level scores are published as Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs, one per metric x subregion, with the manifest carrying each raster’s URL and its build-time rescale range. The map reads those directly.

This replaced a tile factory that ran SQL against the database on every tile request. The rasters were verified byte-identical to the factory’s output before the switch. They are stored without overviews deliberately: the renderer decimates from full resolution per request, so a pre-built pyramid would disagree with it at low zoom.

12.8.4 Interactive maps

The Atlas presents the results, rendering any published release rather than being frozen to one. It has two lenses:

  • Scores — composite and per-category sensitivity by zone, with flower plots and component tables (see Chapter 23)
  • Species — individual and merged distribution models with regulatory status (see Chapter 23)

Substitute the version in the ?ver= query parameter (e.g. ?ver=v3) to view that release (a restricted pre-release is reached only on the review host, at /{version}/atlas/). The separate Scores and Species Shiny apps that earlier editions of this book described are retired, and their links redirect to the Atlas. See Chapter 21.

12.9 Implementation

Scoring runs in the score_zones, score_cell_metrics and score_zone_metrics workflows (Chapter 18). Whole-pipeline equivalence between releases is checked by msens::pra_score_delta, which compares Program-Area scores on the species the two releases share.
