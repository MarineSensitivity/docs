6  v11 — a robust national release

v11 is the release that makes the across-the-nation score of v10 (Chapter 5) robust. It keeps both of v10’s scores, the within-ecoregion one and the distribution share, with the same keys, the same reporting floor and the same composite, and changes what goes into them. Every change is a flag whose control value is off, and with every flag off the pipeline reproduces v10:

  • primary productivity integrated over the whole year, so a polar month without light counts as zero rather than being left out;
  • our own distribution models for the sea turtles, merged by the rule of the species listed by Distinct Population Segment, with their extinction risk beside the value rather than inside it;
  • an occurrence rule: a species is removed from an ecoregion where it has no record under adequate search effort, and down-weighted where it is recorded at a rate below its rate elsewhere;
  • survey density models for seabirds and cetaceans folded into the merge inside each survey’s own domain, calibrated to the surface they replace, with the 2023 Atlantic seabird models;
  • single counting: a merged value is the suitability alone, so extinction risk enters a cell’s score once, as the weight;
  • the component labels Flora and Productivity.

Unlike v10, which changed only the scoring, v11 adds ingests and re-runs the merge and both scores, so the within-ecoregion scores move too.

ImportantServed on the review host only

v11 is a restricted pre-release. It is served only through the signed-in review host: the Atlas (Scores lens, Species lens) and this documentation. It is never what a reader gets by default, and promoting it is a separate decision.

6.3 The rules

The merge and the scoring that the rules below change are described in Chapter 11 (the order the stages run in: Section 11.2) and the Scoring chapter (Section 12.4). The definitions are repeated here so that this page can be read on its own.

6.3.1 Productivity over the whole year

Every release before v11 averages the satellite net primary productivity (Section 8.5) over the months that have a retrieval. In the Bering Sea and the Arctic a winter month has none (polar night, ice), so a “year” there was the mean of the bloom months alone, and the Arctic Program Areas carried productivity well above what is measured in situ. Under a within-ecoregion rescale the error barely mattered; against the national average it does. v11 integrates the year: for cell \(c\), year \(y\), month \(m\) with mean daily rate \(r_{c,m,y}\) and \(d_m\) days,

\[ \bar r_{c,y} = \frac{1}{D_y} \sum_{m=1}^{12} d_m \, r_{c,m,y}, \qquad r_{c,m,y} = 0 \text{ for a month without a retrieval} \tag{6.1}\]

where \(D_y\) is the number of days in the year (366 in a leap year). A cell never observed stays empty. The annual rates are then averaged over the years of the record as before. The rule is msens::vgpm_annual_integrate().

6.3.2 Sea turtles: our own models, on the DPS rule

Two flags change the sea turtles, and they are measured separately.

Suitability (TURTLE_SUIT=ogm). The suitability of the 6 sea turtles comes from our own distribution models (dataset og), fitted with the OBIS / MPA Europe pipeline (Principe and Provoost 2026) to occurrence records on the global 0.05° grid: an ensemble of three algorithms, the median kept continuous as probability × 100, its thresholds recorded and not applied. Inside the footprint of these models it replaces AquaMaps, the way AquaX replaced AquaMaps in v9.

Rule (TURTLE_RULE=dps). Every release since v4b merged the turtles by multiplying extinction risk into the value, \(\max(1, \mathrm{round}(\text{er}_c \cdot \text{suit}_c / 100))\), so their drawn surface was a weight rather than a distribution. v11 merges them like the species listed by Distinct Population Segment (msens::dps_sql(), the rule v9 introduced for the NMFS DPS species, which v10 did not carry and v11 carries again for 19 species): the value is the suitability inside the extinction-risk footprint, the per-cell risk (the SWOT and NMFS DPS surface, with critical habitat joining the footprint at its own status) is kept beside it, and the score multiplies the two. Where a model covers the cell a turtle still scores risk times suitability, now without the rounding and the floor of 1; a footprint cell no model covers takes the fill of Section 6.3.3 instead of the value 1; and the habitat and the risk are kept as two surfaces.

In v11, 112 scored species carry a per-cell extinction risk (taxon.er_mode = 'cell'), 6 of them sea turtles, and 17,003 carry one risk score for the whole species (taxon); none is premultiplied any more.

6.3.3 Single counting

From v4 through v10 a range cell of a species with an expert range is valued \(\max(\text{er}_s, \text{suit}_{s,c})\): the governing extinction-risk score is the floor of the merged value, and the score multiplies by the same risk again, so a range cell no model covers scores \(\text{er}_s^2/100\) and the risk shapes the distribution as well as weighting it. v11 counts the risk once (SHARE_BASIS=cell). For species \(s\) and range cell \(c\):

\[ v_{s,c} = \begin{cases} \text{suit}_{s,c} & \text{if a suitability model covers } c \\ \bar{v}_s = \operatorname{mean}_{c' \in M_s} \text{suit}_{s,c'} & \text{if not, and the species has modelled range cells } M_s \neq \varnothing \\ 100 & \text{if the species has no modelled range cell} \end{cases} \tag{6.2}\]

and the cell score is \(w_{s,c}\, v_{s,c} / 100\) with \(w\) the governing er_score, or the per-cell risk for a species that carries one. The fill of an unmodelled range cell (RANGE_FILL=mean) is the species’ own mean, so it depends neither on the risk score nor on a ceiling; for a surveyed species the mean includes its calibrated survey cells. The rule is msens::merge_sql(basis = "cell", fill = "mean"), and msens::dps_sql(fill = "mean") for the species with a per-cell risk. AquaMaps-only species were single-counted already and are unchanged.

6.3.4 Where the record places a species

v7c, a patch of v7, removed sea turtles from the East Bering Sea and the North Atlantic right whale from the Gulf of America by a hand-built evidence table (Chapter 4). v10 carries no such exclusion. v11 applies one rule to every scored species instead, judged per species and ecoregion on 0.5° blocks of OBIS records (msens::occurrence_rule()): a block is searched if it holds a record of the species’ effort group or of the species itself, and the species’ rate in the ecoregion (blocks with its record over blocks searched) is tested against its rate in its other ecoregions.

  • The gate (OCC_RULE). An ecoregion where none of the searched blocks holds a record, and the absence is significant against the rate elsewhere, loses the species: its cells there are dropped from the merged surface, not set to zero. Critical habitat and core areas are never dropped, a narrow-range species is never dropped, and an ecoregion that was not searched is kept (“unsampled is unknown, not absent”).
  • The weight (REGION_WEIGHT). An ecoregion where the species is recorded, but at a rate below its rate elsewhere, keeps it at a reduced value:

\[ w_{s,e} = \min\!\left(1,\; \frac{\text{rate}_{s,e}}{\text{rate}_{s,\neg e}}\right), \qquad v'_{s,c} = w_{s,e(c)}\, v_{s,c} \tag{6.3}\]

where \(e(c)\) is the ecoregion covering most of cell \(c\) (msens::encounter_weight(), msens::zone_adjust_sql()). Critical habitat is exempt, and the weight scales the value only; the risk layer is never adjusted. Under the within-ecoregion rescale a weight common to every species of a component across a whole ecoregion would cancel, which is why v7c dropped rather than weighted; the share has no rescale, so the weight reaches the score.

6.3.5 Survey densities

Three sets of survey density models enter the merge (FOLD_DENSITY): NOAA SEFSC cetacean and sea-turtle models for the Gulf of America (gm), NOAA NCCOS seabird models for the Pacific and the Atlantic (nc), and NOAA SWFSC cetacean models for the California Current (cce). Each enters as its annual surface, mapped onto 1–100 by the population percentile; interval (monthly or seasonal) surfaces enter no score. The Atlantic seabird models are the 2023 set (NC_ATL_SOURCE=2023), the MDAT Avian Relative Density v3 package of Winship et al. (2023), monthly on 10 km cells, which replaces the 2018 set.

In v11, 94 species have an annual density surface (cce 13, gm 13, nc 71); 5 of them are sea turtles or DPS-listed species, which keep their own rule and leave their density rows out, so 89 merge by the density rule.

Inside its own survey domain a species’ density surface replaces its suitability; the domain joins its range footprint, and the species merges by the DPS rule with its governing er_score as the floor under the per-cell risk, so an Endangered whale’s survey pattern shows beside its risk instead of being painted a flat 100. A survey knows the pattern inside its domain and nothing about how much of the species the domain holds, so its values are calibrated to the surface they replace (FOLD_DENSITY_CAL=suit, msens::density_cal_factors_sql()):

\[ f_s = \frac{\operatorname{mean}_{c \in P_s} \text{prior}_{s,c}}{\operatorname{mean}_{c \in P_s} \text{dens}_{s,c}}, \qquad \text{dens}'_{s,c} = \min(100,\; f_s\, \text{dens}_{s,c}) \tag{6.4}\]

where \(P_s\) is the set of domain cells that had a prior value, and the prior is the suitability where a suitability model covers the cell and the governing er_score where only a range does. A species with no prior anywhere keeps \(f_s = 1\) and is flagged.

The factors are computed for every species with an annual density surface (94): 88 are calibrated, the median factor is 0.453 (range 0.0194 to 5.74), and 64 are scaled down (density_cal_factors_suit.csv).

6.3.6 Component labels

The scored components and their keys are unchanged. Two labels change (COMPONENTS_V11, msens::components_registry()): Primary Producers becomes Flora (primary_producer); Primary productivity becomes Productivity (primprod). The taxa called primary producers are seaweeds, seagrasses, mangroves and other algae; the new names keep them apart from the satellite productivity layer. v11’s release_method states the v11 labels.

Each one-flag run is the candidate with one flag back at its control value, so the candidate minus that run is what the flag owns. The runs are made by scripts/attribution_runs.sh and recorded by snapshot_scores.qmd; only the v11 run is the release.

6.6 What v11 does not contain

  • A range constraint for species modelled only by AquaMaps. 14,992 of the 17,115 scored species are modelled by AquaMaps alone, and their footprint is not constrained by an expert range. A rule for it (keep the connected parts of the footprint that hold a good AquaMaps cell or a record) was explored for v11 and is not applied.
  • AquaX. Its models are clipped to US waters and cannot supply the whole-range denominator of the share; no AquaX cell is in v11’s merge.
  • Distribution models for the species that have none, and our own models for any species but the sea turtles.
  • Survey density models not yet ingested, among them the Atlantic cetacean models of Roberts et al. (2016); and every interval (monthly or seasonal) density surface, which no score reads.
  • WoRMS read from the OBIS snapshot. The taxonomy is downloaded as in v10 (WORMS_SOURCE).

6.7 Caveats

  • The occurrence rule is only as good as the record. A gated pair is a species that the record does not place in an ecoregion that was searched for it; a weighted pair is one recorded less often there than elsewhere. Seasonal and coastal stocks, and species whose records sit in a few places, are the hard cases; the rule’s overrides (critical habitat, core areas, narrow-range species, unsearched ecoregions) exist for them, and the full table of pairs is in the workflows repository.
  • The survey calibration fixes the level, not the pattern. A calibrated survey keeps its pattern inside its domain and takes the mass of the surface it replaces, so how much of a species a survey domain holds still comes from the suitability model or the range.
  • The mean fill is a modelling choice. An unmodelled range cell is worth the species’ average modelled cell; it is not evidence that the species is there at that level.
  • The attribution describes runs, not the release. Each one-flag delta is measured on the candidate, before single counting and the calibration were decided, and the flags compound, so the deltas are a guide to which rule moved a score, not a decomposition of the v10 → v11 change.

6.8 Reproducing v11

The rules are msens functions, each guarded by unit tests with one synthetic fixture per rule: vgpm_annual_integrate() (test-productivity.R: twelve months equal the day-weighted mean, six observed months give 100 × days/365), merge_sql(basis, fill) and dps_sql(fill, er_floor) (test-merge.R: both bases, every fill, the defaults reproduce the earlier SQL byte for byte, a density taxon keeps its national floor), occurrence_rule() and occurrence_exclusions() (test-occurrence.R: the East Bering Sea case falls out of the rule, each override), encounter_weight() and zone_adjust_sql() (test-encounter_weight.R, test-exclude.R), density_cal_factors_sql() and density_prior_sql() (test-density_cal.R) and components_registry() (test-components.R).

The pipeline is the workflows repository at ver = v11: scripts/run_version.sh renders every stage in order (--control, every flag off, reproduces v10), scripts/attribution_runs.sh v11 makes the release run with the decided flags and the one-flag runs by name, and snapshot_scores.qmd records each run’s Program-Area scores in data/v11/attribution/.
